2. Feasibility: The E16 case is 0.029 m across, 0.114 m long, with 33.6 N*s, 17.3 N average thrust, a loaded mass of 0.082 kg and a 1.94 s burn. Delays on the shelf are 0, 4, 6 and 8 s. The body ceiling is 0.025 m. (0.029 - 0.025) * 1000 = 4 mm, so the bare case already sticks out past the limit. A mount wall and a body wall would add still more. No tube of 25 mm or less can swallow this motor. The request is not feasible. What fails is the body diameter against the E16 case. The 4.5 m/s landing cap never gets a chance to matter.
3. Size: There is no legal airframe to size. The nearest build that could actually fly would open the tube to about 32 mm so the 29 mm case, a thin mount and a cardboard wall all fit, then hang a chute sized for under 4.5 m/s. That build breaks the diameter rule, so I am not submitting it.
4. Check: Nothing to fly. The diameter comparison already rules the rocket out, and running a trajectory cannot shrink the motor case.
5. Fix: No change inside both constraints repairs this. A 24 mm or 18 mm motor would fit the tube, or the tube would have to grow past 29 mm. Either choice drops one of the two requirements.
6. Uncertainty: The database figure is the case diameter, not a mount outer diameter, so the real packaging gap is wider than those 4 mm, not narrower. Shop tolerance will not close a miss that large.
