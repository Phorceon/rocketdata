2. Feasibility: Each C6 is 8.8 N*s, 4.8 N average, 14.1 N peak, 1.83 s burn, 18 mm by 0.07 m, 0.0231 kg, delays 0, 3, 5 and 7 s. Three of them are 3 * 8.8 = 26.4 N*s and 3 * 0.0231 = 0.0693 kg of motor. The 3:1 rule on average thrust allows at most 4.8 / (3 * 9.81) = 0.163 kg on the pad, so the airframe has to stay near 0.09 kg. At a 0.15 kg guess, ideal speed is 26.4 / 0.15 = 176 m/s and ideal height (26.4 / 0.15)^2 / (2 * 9.81) = 1580 m, well above 640 m before drag. A slim stack can still overshoot; a heavy one breaks the thrust rule. I will build light and correct whichever way the flight goes.
3. Size: Thrust-to-weight at 0.15 kg is 4.8 / (0.15 * 9.81) = 3.26. Average acceleration on the 2.44 m rail is (4.8 - 0.15 * 9.81) / 0.15 = 22.2 m/s^2, so sqrt(2 * 22.2 * 2.44) = 10.4 m/s, short of 15 m/s. The 14.1 N spike, if it lasted the whole rail, would be (14.1 - 0.15 * 9.81) / 0.15 = 84.2 m/s^2 and sqrt(2 * 84.2 * 2.44) = 20.3 m/s. Reality sits between those two. For a 0.04 kg section at 4 m/s and Cd 0.8, area is 2 * 0.04 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 4^2) = 0.050 m^2, diameter sqrt(4 * 0.050 / 3.1416) = 0.25 m. A 0.30 m chute on each stage covers the 4.57 m/s cap. Sustainer delay 5 s puts ejection near 1.83 * 3 + 5 = 10.5 s, a plausible apogee time for this altitude.
4. Draft: 22 mm cardboard throughout so the stages butt together. Sustainer: 0.06 m polystyrene ogive, 0.16 m tube, C6-5, three small balsa fins. Middle and booster tubes are 0.13 m, also C6-5, separating at burnout with the upper stage lighting at burnout. Booster fins are larger (root 0.07 m, span 0.04 m) because the stack's CP has to come from the tail; upper fins stay small so they do not pull CP forward. Mounts are 0.019 m cardboard with a 0.4 mm wall, which clears the 0.018 m case inside a 0.021 m tube ID. Each stage gets a 0.30 m thin poly chute on the ejection charge. Conditions are the stated 4.47 m/s wind and 2.44 m rail.
