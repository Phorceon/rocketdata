2. Feasibility: The E12 is 27.1 N*s and 24 mm, a bit light for a 762 m minimum once drag is real. The F44W is 42.2 N*s but it burns in 0.82 s, so most of the speed arrives at once and drag takes a big cut. The Estes F15 is 49.6 N*s, 14.5 N average, 25.3 N peak, 3.42 s burn, 29 mm by 0.114 m, 0.103 kg, delays 0, 4, 6 and 8 s. That long burn is the simple way to clear 762 m. Single stage.
3. Size: Guess 0.18 kg with the motor in. Thrust-to-weight 14.5 / (0.18 * 9.81) = 8.2. On the 1.5 m rail, (14.5 - 0.18 * 9.81) / 0.18 = 70.7 m/s^2 and sqrt(2 * 70.7 * 1.5) = 14.6 m/s using average thrust, just under 15 m/s. The 25.3 N peak should cover that, but it is the check I trust least. Burnout mass 0.18 - 0.06 = 0.12 kg. A 5 m/s chute at Cd 0.8 needs 2 * 0.12 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 5^2) = 0.096 m^2, diameter sqrt(4 * 0.096 / 3.1416) = 0.35 m. I will use 0.46 m so the landing sits under 6 m/s with some cloth to spare. Delay 8 s, because ejection at 3.42 + 8 = 11.4 s lets a flight this high coast instead of popping early and giving back altitude.
4. Draft: Beginner airframe, 34 mm cardboard, 0.45 m tube, wall 0.8 mm, polystyrene ogive 0.12 m. Cardboard mount 0.031 m outside, wall 0.7 mm, length 0.12 m, so the 0.029 m case fits. Three balsa fins, root 0.08 m, tip 0.035 m, span 0.055 m, sweep 0.04 m, 3 mm thick, 10 mm forward of the tail. Thin poly chute 0.46 m on the ejection charge. Estes F15-8. Default 1.5 m rail and 2 m/s wind.
