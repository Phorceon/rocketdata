2. Feasibility: The C6 is 8.8 N*s, 4.8 N average, 14.1 N peak, 1.83 s, 18 mm, 0.0231 kg, delays 0, 3, 5, 7. The D12 is 16.8 N*s and 0.0426 kg. Double the impulse on a rocket this small would clear 310 m by a wide margin unless I made it heavy on purpose. The C6 is the closer match. A 29 mm tube, a step wider than a minimum-diameter 18 mm mount, should sit near the target rather than far above it.
3. Size: Liftoff guess 0.060 kg. Thrust-to-weight 4.8 / (0.060 * 9.81) = 8.2. Rail acceleration (4.8 - 0.060 * 9.81) / 0.060 = 70 m/s^2, so sqrt(2 * 70 * 1.5) = 14.5 m/s on average thrust. The 14.1 N spike is what gets it over 15 m/s. Descent mass about 0.060 - 0.011 = 0.049 kg. At 5 m/s, 2 * 0.049 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 5^2) = 0.039 m^2, and sqrt(4 * 0.039 / 3.1416) = 0.22 m. A 0.30 m chute is enough for 6 m/s. Delay 5 s: burnout near 1.8 s, ejection near 6.8 s, which is a fair guess for a 300 m coast.
