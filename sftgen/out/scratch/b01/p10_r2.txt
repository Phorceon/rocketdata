2. Feasibility: The booster is Cesaroni 21062O3400-P, class O, 21041 N*s, 3421 N average, 98 mm by 1.239 m, 16.842 kg. The sustainer is AeroTech M1297W, class M, 5413.8 N*s, 1275 N, 75 mm by 0.665 m, 4.637 kg. The M1939W came back as class N, so it does not count. Both motors only list a 0 s delay, so both chutes are electronic. Together that is about 26,500 N*s. On a guessed 28 kg stack, liftoff thrust-to-weight is 3421 / (28 * 9.81) = 12.5, and 7,500 m is a reasonable ask rather than a fantasy.
3. Size: Booster empty is about 28 - 11 = 17 kg of which the sustainer leaves, so the booster section itself is near 17 - 7 = 10 kg after the split. Call the falling booster 9.7 kg and the sustainer 3.9 kg. At 5 m/s, the booster needs 2 * 9.7 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 5^2) = 7.8 m^2, diameter sqrt(4 * 7.8 / 3.1416) = 3.2 m. The sustainer needs 2 * 3.9 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 5^2) = 3.1 m^2, diameter sqrt(4 * 3.1 / 3.1416) = 2.0 m. I will use 3.4 m and 2.2 m so both stay under 5.49 m/s. The O case is 0.098 m, so the shared tube is 0.110 m and the booster mount is 0.102 m.
4. Draft: Sustainer on top: fiberglass ogive 0.35 m, 1.0 m tube, M1297W plugged in a 0.080 m mount, 2.2 m chute at apogee, smaller fins (root 0.22 m, span 0.14 m). Booster below, separates at burnout, 1.55 m tube, O3400 plugged in a 0.102 m by 1.28 m mount, 3.4 m chute at apogee, fins root 0.32 m, tip 0.12 m, span 0.22 m, sweep 0.16 m, 6 mm fiberglass. Both tubes 0.110 m, wall 3 mm. Wind 4 m/s and site 1,300 m on the run.
