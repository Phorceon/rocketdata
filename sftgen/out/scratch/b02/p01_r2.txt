2. Feasibility: The K550W is a 54 mm reload with 1,620 N*s, 536 N average thrust and a 3.02 s burn. Loaded mass is 1.49 kg, of which 0.92 kg is propellant, and the only listed delay is 0 s, so I will fly it plugged and open the chute at apogee with an altimeter. The L1000W has more impulse (2,700 N*s) but the motor alone weighs 2.19 kg in the same 54 mm diameter, and the L850W is a 75 mm, 3.67 kg case. The lighter K is the one that can actually get near 2,500 m; the bigger motors mostly haul their own casing. Thrust-to-weight on a roughly 2.2 kg rocket is 536 / (2.2 * 9.81) = 24.8, far above 3, so the short rail is not the binding constraint.

3. Size: Take liftoff mass as 2.2 kg (motor plus a short phenolic airframe, fins and a small altimeter). Weight is 2.2 * 9.81 = 21.6 N, so average acceleration on the rail is (536 - 21.6) / 2.2 = 234 m/s^2 and rail exit is about sqrt(2 * 234 * 1.5) = 26.5 m/s. Burnout mass is 2.2 - 0.92 = 1.28 kg. A 5 m/s descent at Cd 0.8 needs canopy area 2 * 1.28 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 5^2) = 1.03 m^2, diameter sqrt(4 * 1.03 / 3.1416) = 1.15 m. I will use a 1.2 m ripstop chute so the landing sits under 6 m/s with a little margin. Coast time on a flight this high is well past any motor delay, which is why deployment is at apogee rather than on an ejection charge.

4. Draft: 62 mm phenolic tube, 0.72 m long, with a hollow polystyrene ogive and three plywood fins. The 54 mm mount is sized to the K550W case. A 30 g altimeter sits at the top of the tube. Flying it in 4 m/s wind on the assumed 1.5 m rail.
