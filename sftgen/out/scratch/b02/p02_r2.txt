2. Feasibility: Every one of those motors is 29 mm, which is inside the 38 mm cap. The H128W gives 178 N*s and 153 N for 1.16 s, loaded mass 0.20 kg, and its only delay is 14 s. The H180W has 234 N*s but only a 6 s delay, which would open the chute while a 650 m flight is still climbing. The I200W is 327 N*s with a plugged delay and would overshoot a "roughly 650 m" target in any light airframe. The G80T at 136 N*s is the one most likely to fall short. I will build around the H128W and use its 14 s ejection.

3. Size: Guess liftoff mass 0.32 kg for a short 42 mm cardboard rocket plus the motor. Thrust-to-weight is 153 / (0.32 * 9.81) = 48.7. Weight is 0.32 * 9.81 = 3.14 N, so rail acceleration is (153 - 3.14) / 0.32 = 468 m/s^2 and rail exit is about sqrt(2 * 468 * 1.5) = 37.5 m/s. Burnout mass is 0.32 - 0.094 = 0.226 kg. To descend at 4 m/s, under the 4.57 m/s cap, canopy area is 2 * 0.226 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 4^2) = 0.283 m^2, diameter sqrt(4 * 0.283 / 3.1416) = 0.600 m. A 0.70 m chute gives some margin because "softer than 15 ft/s" has no slack.

4. Draft: 42 mm cardboard tube, 0.50 m long, hollow polystyrene ogive, three balsa fins, 29 mm mount for the H128W-14. Checking it on the default 1.5 m rail and 2 m/s wind.
