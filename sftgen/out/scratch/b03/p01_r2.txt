2. Feasibility: the H128W is only 177.8 N*s, and a 29 mm rocket on that motor usually tops out well under 1,500 m, so it is the wrong class for a 1,750 m minimum. The I200W is 326.8 N*s but only 29 mm in diameter and has a plugged delay (0 s), so recovery would have to be electronic. The I300T gives 436.8 N*s at 295.9 N average, fits a 38 mm case (0.038 m by 0.25 m, 0.4405 kg loaded), and offers 6, 10 and 14 s delays. The J350W is 669.2 N*s with no delay grain at all. The I300T is enough impulse for the altitude and still has a motor ejection charge, so I will fly that.
3. Size: I am guessing 0.75 kg at liftoff, motor included. Thrust-to-weight = 295.9 / (0.75 * 9.81) = 40.2, far above 3. Weight on the pad is 0.75 * 9.81 = 7.36 N, so average acceleration on the rod is (295.9 - 7.36) / 0.75 = 385 m/s^2 and the 1 m rod gives sqrt(2 * 385 * 1) = 27.7 m/s, clear of 15 m/s. Propellant is 0.222 kg, so the descent mass is about 0.75 - 0.222 = 0.53 kg. For 5 m/s under a Cd 0.8 chute, area = 2 * 0.53 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 5^2) = 0.424 m^2, and diameter = sqrt(4 * 0.424 / 3.1416) = 0.73 m. I will use a 0.91 m canopy so a heavier build still lands under 6 m/s. Burn is 1.47 s; adding the 14 s delay puts ejection at 1.47 + 14 = 15.5 s, which should be near apogee for a flight of this height.
4. Draft: 0.14 m ogive fiberglass nose, 2 mm wall, on a 0.70 m kraft-phenolic tube, 0.044 m outer diameter and 1 mm wall. Motor mount is 0.041 m outside diameter, 0.26 m long, 1 mm wall, holding the I300T on the 14 s delay with 10 mm overhang. Three fiberglass fins: root 0.10 m, tip 0.045 m, span 0.060 m, sweep 0.050 m, 3 mm thick, at the tail. Chute is 0.91 m ripstop, Cd 0.8, on motor ejection. Flying it at 3 m/s wind on the 1 m rod.
