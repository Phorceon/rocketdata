2. Feasibility: C6 and C11 are both 8.8 N*s. The C6 is an 18 mm case (0.018 m by 0.07 m, 0.0231 kg, 4.8 N average) and the C11 is 24 mm (0.024 m, 11.3 N average, 0.0353 kg). The D12 is also 24 mm but 16.8 N*s, which on a light rocket would sail well past 375 m. The E12 is 27.1 N*s and still 24 mm, so it is legal on diameter and far too much impulse. A C6 in a slim cardboard tube is the closest match. All four motors are at or under 24 mm.
3. Size: guessed liftoff mass 0.040 kg. Thrust-to-weight = 4.8 / (0.040 * 9.81) = 12.2. Pad weight is 0.040 * 9.81 = 0.392 N, so rail acceleration is (4.8 - 0.392) / 0.040 = 110 m/s^2 and exit speed is sqrt(2 * 110 * 1.5) = 18.2 m/s. That clears 15 m/s on the average thrust, and the C6 spike should add more. Propellant is 0.011 kg, descent mass 0.040 - 0.011 = 0.029 kg. A 5 m/s chute at Cd 0.8 needs 2 * 0.029 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 5^2) = 0.0232 m^2, diameter sqrt(4 * 0.0232 / 3.1416) = 0.17 m. I will fly a 0.30 m chute so the landing stays under 6 m/s if the rocket is heavier than this guess. Burn is 1.83 s; a 5 s delay ejects near 7 s, about when a flight this size reaches apogee.
