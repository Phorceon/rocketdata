2. Feasibility: J350W is 669.2 N*s at 445.4 N average, a 38 mm case (0.038 m by 0.337 m, 0.651 kg) with no delay grains, so recovery has to be an altimeter at apogee. J420R is similar, 651.2 N*s, and also plugged. J800T is 1,264.4 N*s in a 54 mm case and would likely sail past 2,750 m. K550W is 1,624.9 N*s, heavier still. The J350W is the smallest of these that can plausibly reach the altitude in a 54 mm tube, and I will fly the closest legal wind, 8.9 m/s, not the requested 12 m/s.
3. Size: guessed liftoff mass 0.95 kg. Thrust-to-weight = 445.4 / (0.95 * 9.81) = 47.8. Pad weight is 0.95 * 9.81 = 9.32 N, so rail acceleration is (445.4 - 9.32) / 0.95 = 459 m/s^2 and exit speed is sqrt(2 * 459 * 1.5) = 37.1 m/s. Propellant is 0.361 kg, descent mass 0.95 - 0.361 = 0.59 kg. At 6 m/s and Cd 0.8 the canopy is 2 * 0.59 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 6^2) = 0.328 m^2, diameter sqrt(4 * 0.328 / 3.1416) = 0.65 m. I will use 0.85 m. There is no ejection charge, so the chute is set to apogee.
