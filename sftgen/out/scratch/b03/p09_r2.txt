2. Feasibility: the F15 is only 49.6 N*s and 14.5 N average, soft for 700 m. The G64W is 118 N*s and the G80T is 135.6 N*s, both likely to fly well past the 5% band in a light tube. The Estes F50 is 76.8 N*s at 55.8 N average, a 29 mm case (0.029 m by 0.098 m, 0.0836 kg) with a single 6 s delay. That impulse is the closest match, and the thrust is healthy on a 1.5 m rail.
3. Size: guessed liftoff mass 0.18 kg. Thrust-to-weight = 55.8 / (0.18 * 9.81) = 31.6. Pad weight 0.18 * 9.81 = 1.77 N, rail acceleration (55.8 - 1.77) / 0.18 = 300 m/s^2, exit speed sqrt(2 * 300 * 1.5) = 30 m/s. Propellant is 0.038 kg, descent mass 0.18 - 0.038 = 0.14 kg. Recovery is the priority, so I size the chute for 4.5 m/s rather than 6: area 2 * 0.14 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 4.5^2) = 0.138 m^2, diameter sqrt(4 * 0.138 / 3.1416) = 0.42 m. I will use 0.55 m. Burn is 1.37 s, so the 6 s delay ejects near 7.4 s, which may be a little early for a 700 m coast.
4. Draft: 0.12 m ogive polystyrene nose on a 0.50 m cardboard tube, outer diameter 0.041 m, wall 0.8 mm. Mount 0.032 m outside, 0.11 m long, Estes F50 with the 6 s delay. Three balsa fins, root 0.080 m, tip 0.035 m, span 0.055 m, sweep 0.040 m, 3 mm thick. One 0.55 m polyethylene chute on ejection. Wind 6 m/s.
