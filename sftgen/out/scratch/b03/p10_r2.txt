2. Feasibility: the Estes D12 is 16.8 N*s at 10.4 N average, a 24 mm case, 0.07 m long, 0.0426 kg, with 0, 3, 5 and 7 s delays. The E12 is 27.1 N*s in the same 24 mm diameter and would fly higher than a first rocket needs. The D12 is the one I would hand a new flyer: enough impulse to beat 400 m, and the 5 s delay matches a flight of this size. The E stays on the shelf.
3. Size: guessed liftoff mass 0.12 kg. Thrust-to-weight = 10.4 / (0.12 * 9.81) = 8.83. Pad weight 0.12 * 9.81 = 1.18 N, rail acceleration (10.4 - 1.18) / 0.12 = 76.8 m/s^2, and the 2 m rail gives sqrt(2 * 76.8 * 2) = 17.5 m/s on the average thrust. The D12 spike should clear 15 m/s. Propellant is 0.021 kg, descent mass 0.12 - 0.021 = 0.099 kg. For 4.5 m/s, under the 5.5 m/s cap, area = 2 * 0.099 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 4.5^2) = 0.0979 m^2, diameter sqrt(4 * 0.0979 / 3.1416) = 0.35 m. I will use 0.45 m so a heavier build still lands slowly.
