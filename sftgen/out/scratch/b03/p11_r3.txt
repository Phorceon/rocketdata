4. Check: the light rocket is a normal flight and it is nowhere near 65,000 ft. The simulator gives 1,664.1 m. Against about 19,800 m that is 19800 / 1664.1 = 11.9 times too low, or (19800 - 1664.1) / 19800 = 91.6% short. The rest is fine: margin 1.3 cal at the rail and through the burn, landing 4.2 m/s, rail exit 43.6 m/s, thrust-to-weight 52.0. The chute opens at 12.3 s, on the 12.31 s apogee. Mass is 0.4 kg. Mach 1.79, with the supersonic warning. That drag is exactly why the vacuum estimate of tens of kilometres collapsed.
5. Fix: no airframe change. 1,664.1 m still misses about 19,800 m, and shaving fins or grams cannot multiply the altitude by 12. One I205 in a single stage cannot do this. The request is infeasible.
6. Uncertainty: the supersonic drag model is already flagged, so 1,664.1 m could be off by hundreds of metres. It cannot be off by 18 km. A lighter glass nose or a thinner tube might add a few hundred metres and would still be an I-class flight. Landing at 4.2 m/s assumes the 0.55 m chute. The motor really is 346.4 N*s; that number is not the uncertain part.
