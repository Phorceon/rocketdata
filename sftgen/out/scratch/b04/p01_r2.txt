2. Feasibility: the B6 is only 4.3 N*s and the catalog lists a single 0 s delay, so the chute would open at burnout and the rocket would never coast to 229 m. The B4 lookup returned 5.1 N*s but no ejection delays at all. The C6 is 8.8 N*s, average thrust 4.8 N, burn 1.83 s, and it has a 5 s delay. On a slim 25 mm tube that impulse usually clears 300 m; a standard BT-55 (about 34 mm) adds enough drag that 229 m is a fair first guess, and a beginner can buy the tube.
3. Size: guessed liftoff mass 0.075 kg. Thrust-to-weight = 4.8 / (0.075 * 9.81) = 6.52, above 3. Average acceleration on the rail is (4.8 - 0.736) / 0.075 = 54.2 m/s^2, so rail exit is about sqrt(2 * 54.2 * 1.5) = 12.8 m/s using average thrust. That sits under the 15 m/s gate, but the C6 spikes to 14.1 N, so the real exit speed should clear it. Descent mass is 0.075 - 0.011 = 0.064 kg. For 5 m/s at Cd 0.8, area = 2 * 0.064 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 5^2) = 0.0513 m^2, diameter sqrt(4 * 0.0513 / 3.1416) = 0.255 m. A 0.35 m plastic chute gives margin under 6 m/s. Burnout is at 1.83 s and a coast of a few seconds fits the 5 s delay.
