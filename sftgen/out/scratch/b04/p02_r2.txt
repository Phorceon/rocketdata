2. Feasibility: asking for G80 returned the AeroTech G80T, 135.6 N*s, 80.6 N average, 1.68 s burn, 29 mm, delays 4, 7 and 10 s. The H128W is also 29 mm but 177.8 N*s with only a 14 s delay, which is more impulse than a waiver-capped rocket needs. The H73J is 38 mm, so it fails the motor requirement. A G80T in a 54 mm blue-tube airframe should stay under 900 m; a G64W at 118 N*s is the fallback if this one climbs through the waiver.
3. Size: tube mass is about pi * 0.0525 * 0.70 * 0.0015 * 1300 = 0.225 kg. Nose, fins and chute add roughly 0.12 kg and the motor is 0.129 kg, so liftoff is 0.225 + 0.12 + 0.129 = 0.474 kg. Thrust-to-weight = 80.6 / (0.474 * 9.81) = 17.3. Rail acceleration (80.6 - 4.65) / 0.474 = 160 m/s^2, so exit speed sqrt(2 * 160 * 1.5) = 21.9 m/s. Descent mass 0.474 - 0.069 = 0.405 kg. At 4.5 m/s and Cd 0.8, area = 2 * 0.405 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 4.5^2) = 0.400 m^2, diameter sqrt(4 * 0.400 / 3.1416) = 0.714 m. I will fit a 0.90 m chute so a heavier build still lands under 5.5 m/s. Burnout is at 1.68 s; a coast of around 8 s fits the 10 s delay better than the 7 s one.
4. Draft: 54 mm blue tube, 0.70 m long, fiberglass ogive, three birch-ply fins, 29 mm phenolic mount and a G80T-10. One ripstop chute on motor ejection. Default 2 m/s wind and a 1.5 m rail.
