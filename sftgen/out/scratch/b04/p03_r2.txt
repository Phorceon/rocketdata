2. Feasibility: the catalog's AeroTech L1520 is the L1520T, 3769 N*s, average thrust 1511 N, burn 2.49 s, 75 mm by 0.518 m, loaded mass 3.62 kg. The only listed delay is 0 s, so a motor ejection would open the chute at burnout and throw away the coast. For maximum altitude the motor flies plugged and an electronic chute opens at apogee. Impulse is far past the 2740 m floor on any reasonable single-stage airframe; the design problem is drag, stability and a soft landing.
3. Size: a near-minimum-diameter fiberglass tube, 81 mm OD and 1.05 m long, is about pi * 0.0795 * 1.05 * 0.0015 * 1850 = 0.728 kg. Nose, fins, mount and a large chute add about 0.80 kg, so liftoff is 0.728 + 0.80 + 3.62 = 5.15 kg. Thrust-to-weight = 1511 / (5.15 * 9.81) = 29.9. Rail acceleration (1511 - 50.5) / 5.15 = 284 m/s^2, exit speed sqrt(2 * 284 * 1.5) = 29.2 m/s. Descent mass 5.15 - 1.77 = 3.38 kg. At 4.2 m/s and Cd 0.8, area = 2 * 3.38 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 4.2^2) = 3.84 m^2, diameter sqrt(4 * 3.84 / 3.1416) = 2.21 m. Fin span 0.12 m is 0.12 / 0.081 = 1.5 calibers, which should put the CP behind a motor that is most of the mass.
4. Draft: 81 mm fiberglass airframe, haack nose, three carbon fins, 75 mm mount, L1520T plugged, 2.2 m ripstop chute at apogee. Defaults: 2 m/s wind, 1.5 m rail.
