2. Feasibility: the I300T is class I, 436.8 N*s, 295.9 N average, 1.47 s burn, 38 mm, with delays of 6, 10 and 14 s. That stays under the J cap. The I200W and I285R only list a 0 s delay, so they would eject at burnout. 437 N*s in a slim rocket can exceed 1,200 m, so the airframe is a 76 mm blue tube, wide enough to add drag while the motor still lifts it.
3. Size: guessed liftoff mass 1.20 kg, motor included. Thrust-to-weight = 295.9 / (1.20 * 9.81) = 25.1. Rail acceleration (295.9 - 11.8) / 1.20 = 237 m/s^2, exit speed sqrt(2 * 237 * 1.5) = 26.7 m/s. Descent mass 1.20 - 0.222 = 0.978 kg. At 4.0 m/s and Cd 0.8, area = 2 * 0.978 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 4^2) = 1.22 m^2, diameter sqrt(4 * 1.22 / 3.1416) = 1.25 m. A 1.40 m chute leaves margin under 4.5 m/s. Burnout at 1.47 s plus a coast of several seconds fits the 10 s delay.
4. Draft: 76 mm blue tube, 0.85 m long, fiberglass ogive, three elliptical birch fins, 38 mm phenolic mount, I300T-10, one 1.40 m chute on motor ejection. Default wind and rail.
