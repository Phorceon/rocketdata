2. Feasibility: two D12 motors would be 16.8 * 2 = 33.6 N*s, about twice the impulse a 95 m flight wants. Two C6 motors are only 4.8 * 2 = 9.6 N of average thrust, so a rocket heavy enough to stay near 95 m would fall under a 3:1 thrust-to-weight. Two C11 motors are 8.8 * 2 = 17.6 N*s and 11.3 * 2 = 22.6 N, still class C, with 3, 5 and 7 s delays. That is the cluster that can both lift and stay low. They are 24 mm, so the two mounts need a tube wider than about 52 mm.
3. Size: guessed liftoff mass 0.32 kg. Thrust-to-weight = 22.6 / (0.32 * 9.81) = 7.20. Rail acceleration (22.6 - 3.14) / 0.32 = 60.8 m/s^2, exit speed sqrt(2 * 60.8 * 1.5) = 13.5 m/s on average thrust. The C11 spikes to 21.7 N each, so the real exit speed should clear 15 m/s. Descent mass 0.32 - 0.024 = 0.296 kg. At 4.5 m/s and Cd 0.8, area = 2 * 0.296 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 4.5^2) = 0.293 m^2, diameter sqrt(4 * 0.293 / 3.1416) = 0.611 m. A 0.70 m chute stays under 5.49 m/s. Burn is only 0.78 s, so a 5 s delay ejects near 6 s, about right for a short hop.
4. Draft: 66 mm cardboard tube, 0.45 m long, small ogive nose, four balsa fins, a two-motor 24 mm cluster of C11-5. One chute on ejection. Default wind and a 1.5 m rail.
