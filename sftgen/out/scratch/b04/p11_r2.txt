2. Feasibility: the D12 is 16.8 N*s, 10.4 N, burn 1.61 s, 24 mm, and 5 s is a listed delay. The C6 is 8.8 N*s, 4.8 N, burn 1.83 s, 18 mm, also with a 5 s delay. Together that is 16.8 + 8.8 = 25.6 N*s. Liftoff thrust is only the D12. On a guessed 0.18 kg stack, thrust-to-weight = 10.4 / (0.18 * 9.81) = 5.89, above 3. The C6 does not light until 5 s after D12 burnout, which is the D12-5 ejection.
3. Size: rail acceleration (10.4 - 1.77) / 0.18 = 47.9 m/s^2, exit speed sqrt(2 * 47.9 * 1.5) = 12.0 m/s on average thrust. The D12 spikes to 29.7 N, so the real exit should clear 15 m/s. Sustainer descent mass is about 0.08 kg. At 4 m/s and Cd 0.8, area = 2 * 0.08 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 4^2) = 0.100 m^2, diameter sqrt(4 * 0.100 / 3.1416) = 0.357 m. A 0.40 m sustainer chute and a 0.36 m booster chute should both stay under 4.57 m/s. The C6-5 then opens the upper chute about 1.83 + 5 = 6.8 s after staging.
4. Draft: both stages are 42 mm cardboard. Sustainer on top with the C6-5, ignition delay 5 s, and its own fins and chute. Booster below with the D12-5, separation on ejection, its own fins and chute. Site altitude 1300 m, wind left at 2 m/s.
