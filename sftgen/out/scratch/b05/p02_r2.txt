2. Feasibility: C6 and C11 are both 8.8 N*s. The C6 averages 4.8 N for 1.83 s in an 18 mm case; the C11 averages 11.3 N but burns in 0.78 s and is 24 mm, so the airframe gets fatter for the same impulse. The D12 is 16.8 N*s, and 16.8 / 8.8 = 1.9 times the C, which on this size of rocket would clear 400 m. I'll use the C6.
3. Size: guessed liftoff mass 0.055 kg. Thrust-to-weight = 4.8 / (0.055 * 9.81) = 8.9. Weight is 0.055 * 9.81 = 0.54 N, so rail acceleration is (4.8 - 0.54) / 0.055 = 77 m/s^2 and rail exit is sqrt(2 * 77 * 1.5) = 15.2 m/s before the ignition spike. Propellant is about 0.011 kg, so descent mass is 0.055 - 0.011 = 0.044 kg. A 115 s flight with about 8 s to apogee leaves 115 - 8 = 107 s of descent, and 381 / 107 = 3.6 m/s. Canopy area at Cd 0.8 is 2 * 0.044 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 3.6^2) = 0.068 m^2, diameter sqrt(4 * 0.068 / 3.1416) = 0.29 m. I'll fit a 0.30 m chute and open it at apogee, because the longest C6 delay is 7 s and that may still be early or late.
4. Draft: 24.8 mm cardboard tube, 0.42 m long, short ogive nose, three balsa fins, 18 mm mount, Estes C6 plugged. Wind in the call is 2.2 m/s.
