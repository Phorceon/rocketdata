2. Feasibility: the B6 is only 4.3 N*s and its only listed delay is 0 s, so it ejects at burnout. The C6 is 8.8 N*s, and 8.8 / 4.3 = 2.0 times the B, at 4.8 N for 1.83 s with delays of 3, 5 and 7 s. The B will not reach 310 m. The C6 will, on a small cardboard rocket.
3. Size: guessed liftoff mass 0.06 kg. Thrust-to-weight = 4.8 / (0.06 * 9.81) = 8.2. Rail acceleration is (4.8 - 0.06 * 9.81) / 0.06 = 70 m/s^2, so rail exit is sqrt(2 * 70 * 1.5) = 14.5 m/s on average thrust. The 14.1 N spike should clear 15 m/s. To be down by 90 s with about 7 s to apogee, descent can take at most 90 - 7 = 83 s, so the sink rate has to be at least 310 / 83 = 3.7 m/s and at most 6 m/s. I'll aim at 5 m/s. Descent mass is about 0.06 - 0.011 = 0.049 kg. Area is 2 * 0.049 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 5^2) = 0.039 m^2, diameter sqrt(4 * 0.039 / 3.1416) = 0.22 m. A 0.24 m chute is a little slower, about 5 * 0.22 / 0.24 = 4.6 m/s, still fast enough to beat 90 s and soft enough to beat 6 m/s.
4. Draft: 24.8 mm cardboard tube, 0.50 m long, ogive nose, three balsa fins, 18 mm mount, Estes C6 with a 5 s delay and a single chute on motor ejection. That is a build someone can glue up without electronics.
