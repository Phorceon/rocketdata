2. Feasibility: G80 lookup returned G80T, 135.6 N*s, 80.6 N, 29 mm. H128W is 177.8 N*s, H180W is 233.7 N*s, I200W is 326.8 N*s, all 29 mm and all under a K. Four G80T motors are 4 * 135.6 = 542 N*s. Four H128W motors are 4 * 177.8 = 711 N*s, about a third more, which is likely past 1,360 m on a light cluster rocket. I200W only lists a 0 s delay and even more impulse. I'll start with four G80T. A 29 mm mount of 0.032 m OD in a cluster of 4 needs about 0.032 * 2.4 = 0.077 m of inside width, so the tube ID has to clear that.
3. Size: guessed liftoff mass 1.3 kg. Thrust is 4 * 80.6 = 322 N, so thrust-to-weight is 322 / (1.3 * 9.81) = 25. Rail acceleration is (322 - 1.3 * 9.81) / 1.3 = 238 m/s^2 and rail exit is sqrt(2 * 238 * 1.5) = 27 m/s. Propellant is about 4 * 0.069 = 0.28 kg, descent mass 1.3 - 0.28 = 1.0 kg. Landing has to be under 4.6 m/s, so I size for 4 m/s. Area is 2 * 1.0 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 4^2) = 1.25 m^2, diameter sqrt(4 * 1.25 / 3.1416) = 1.3 m.
4. Draft: 86 mm blue-tube airframe, four G80T motors in one mount, four plywood fins, 1.3 m chute at apogee. Wind in the call is 4.5 m/s.
