2. Feasibility: E12 is 27.1 N*s at 11.2 N, E9 is 27.9 N*s at only 9.2 N, and E20W is 34.7 N*s. 34.7 / 27.1 = 1.3, so the E20 is the one most likely to punch through 457 m. The E9's lower thrust is worse on a 1.5 m rail. I'll use the E12 and a wider tube than a minimum-diameter 24 mm rocket, because a slim E will clear 457 m. All three are class E, which is the cap.
3. Size: guessed liftoff mass 0.16 kg. Thrust-to-weight is 11.2 / (0.16 * 9.81) = 7.1. Rail acceleration is (11.2 - 0.16 * 9.81) / 0.16 = 60 m/s^2, so average-thrust rail exit is sqrt(2 * 60 * 1.5) = 13 m/s. The 33 N spike should clear 15 m/s. Descent mass is about 0.16 - 0.036 = 0.12 kg. For a 4 m/s landing, under the 4.6 m/s limit, area is 2 * 0.12 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 4^2) = 0.15 m^2 and diameter is sqrt(4 * 0.15 / 3.1416) = 0.44 m. I'll fit a 0.45 m chute at apogee.
4. Draft: 41.6 mm cardboard tube, 0.65 m long, ogive nose, three balsa fins, 24 mm mount, Estes E12 plugged. The extra diameter is what holds the flight under the ceiling.
