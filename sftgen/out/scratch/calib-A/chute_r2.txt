2. Feasibility: the F15 at 49.6 N*s leaves little drag margin for 488 m and its 14.5 N average thrust gives a slow rail exit. The G64W at 118 N*s fits under the 160 N*s cap but on a light airframe it would sail well past the window. The F32T at 56.7 N*s and 34.2 N average is the middle option and the first choice.
3. Size: airframe about 0.18 kg plus the 0.064 kg motor gives liftoff near 0.25 kg. Thrust-to-weight = 34.2 / (0.25 * 9.81) = 13.9, well above 3. Rail exit on the 1.83 m rail: a = (34.2 - 2.45) / 0.25 = 127 m/s^2, so v = sqrt(2 * 127 * 1.83) = 21.6 m/s. Ideal delta-v = 56.7 / 0.25 = 227 m/s; the ballistic ceiling from that is far past 488 m, so whether this lands in the window comes down to drag on a 41.6 mm body at altitude. Descent mass is about 0.25 - 0.026 = 0.22 kg. At 762 m elevation the air density is roughly 1.1 kg/m^3, and a 0.55 m chute (area 0.238 m^2) at Cd 0.8 gives v = sqrt(2 * 0.22 * 9.81 / (1.1 * 0.8 * 0.238)) = 4.5 m/s, under the 5.5 m/s limit. Apogee should come near 7 to 9 s, so the 6 s delay is the first guess.
4. Draft: 41.6 mm card body, 0.55 m long, solid balsa ogive nose, three birch-ply fins, 25.5 mm mount for the 24 mm F32T-6, 0.55 m ripstop chute on motor ejection. Simulating at the stated 1.83 m rail and 762 m site elevation.
