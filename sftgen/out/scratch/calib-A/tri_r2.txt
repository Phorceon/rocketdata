2. Feasibility: the Quest C12 is the answer — 11.9 N average each, so three of them give 3 * 11.9 = 35.7 N on only 3 * 0.0237 = 0.071 kg of motors, and they are still C class at 9.9 N*s apiece. The C11 offers similar thrust but is 24 mm wide and heavier; the C6 and B6 cannot lift a cluster off a 0.91 m rod. Maximum liftoff mass for rail exit is about 35.7 / (124 + 9.81) = 0.267 kg.
3. Size: airframe about 0.10 kg plus motors gives liftoff near 0.175 kg. Thrust-to-weight = 35.7 / (0.175 * 9.81) = 20.8. On the rod a = (35.7 - 1.72) / 0.175 = 194 m/s^2, so v = sqrt(2 * 194 * 0.91) = 18.8 m/s at exit. Ideal delta-v = 29.7 / 0.175 = 170 m/s; a 45 mm airframe burns a lot of that to drag, so the result should land in the few-hundred-metre band around the goal. Three 19.5 mm mounts pack a cluster span of 19.5 * 2.155 = 42.0 mm, which needs a body ID of at least 42 mm — a 45 mm tube with 0.8 mm walls leaves 43.4 mm. Descent mass about 0.175 - 0.0312 = 0.14 kg; a 0.40 m chute at Cd 0.8 gives sqrt(2 * 0.14 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 0.126)) = 4.7 m/s. Delay: three ejection charges fire together, so ignition redundancy is built in; C12 offers 4, 6 and 8 s, and for a ~300 m flight I'll take the 4 s option.
4. Draft: 45 mm card body, 0.50 m long, hollow plastic ogive nose, four balsa fins, triple 19.5 mm mounts on two plywood rings, Quest C12-4s, 0.40 m chute. Simulating on the 0.91 m rod.
