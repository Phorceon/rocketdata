2. Feasibility: the C6 returns 8.8 N*s at 4.8 N average, which is the right band. The B6 only offers a 0 s delay in this database, so it cannot eject a chute, and the D12's 24 mm mount and 42.6 g loaded mass overshoot what a floor of 175 m needs. C6-5 it is.
3. Size: airframe about 0.022 kg plus the 0.0231 kg motor puts liftoff near 0.045 kg. Thrust-to-weight = 4.8 / (0.045 * 9.81) = 10.9, well above 3. On the rail a = (4.8 - 0.44) / 0.045 = 96.9 m/s^2, giving v = sqrt(2 * 96.9 * 1.5) = 17.0 m/s at exit, above the 15 m/s minimum. Ideal delta-v = 8.8 / 0.045 = 196 m/s before gravity and drag losses; on a minimum-diameter airframe that should still land a few hundred metres up. Descent mass is about 0.045 - 0.0108 = 0.034 kg. A 0.30 m chute (area 0.0707 m^2) at Cd 0.8 gives v = sqrt(2 * 0.034 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 0.0707)) = 3.1 m/s. Apogee should come near 6 to 8 s, so the 5 s delay is the first guess.
