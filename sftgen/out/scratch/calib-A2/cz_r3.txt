2. Feasibility: the F67W is the motor I will fly. It gives 62.1 N*s at 73.3 N, burns for 0.85 s, weighs 0.080 kg and is single-use. The F40W carries more impulse, 78.1 N*s, but only 40.3 N and a 0.126 kg case. The G64W is 118 N*s, too much rocket for 650 m. The 10 s flight is still impossible. Falling from rest at 650 m takes sqrt(2 * 650 / 9.81) = 11.5 s even with no drag, and drag makes that fall slower, so the descent alone breaks the cap. I will aim the airframe at about 650 m and a landing under 4.57 m/s, and leave the time as the constraint that cannot be met.
3. Size: cardboard tube pi * 0.038 * 0.0008 * 0.48 * 700 = 0.032 kg. Three balsa fins 3 * 0.5 * (0.10 + 0.04) * 0.06 * 0.0032 * 160 = 0.0065 kg. Nose, mount, rings and chute 0.015 + 0.020 = 0.035 kg. Liftoff 0.032 + 0.0065 + 0.035 + 0.080 = 0.154 kg. Thrust-to-weight 73.3 / (0.154 * 9.81) = 48.5. Rail acceleration (73.3 - 0.154 * 9.81) / 0.154 = 466 m/s^2, and sqrt(2 * 466 * 1.5) = 37.4 m/s on the assumed 1.5 m rail. Propellant is 0.030 kg, so the chute sees 0.154 - 0.030 = 0.124 kg. For 4.0 m/s at Cd 0.8 the area is 2 * 0.124 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 4.0^2) = 0.155 m^2, diameter sqrt(4 * 0.155 / pi) = 0.444 m. A 0.50 m canopy keeps the landing under 4.57 m/s if the built mass is a bit high. The longest delay is 9 s, so ejection is at 0.85 + 9 = 9.85 s. The vacuum climb is 11.5 s, so the charge can be a little early, and 9 s is as late as this motor goes.
