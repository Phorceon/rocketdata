2. Feasibility: three of either motor is 3 * 8.8 = 26.4 N*s. The C6 averages 3 * 4.8 = 14.4 N and the C11 averages 3 * 11.3 = 33.9 N. On a short rod I want the C11. It is a 24 mm motor, so the three of them weigh 3 * 0.0353 = 0.106 kg and all of that sits in the tail. A C6 cluster would be lighter but softer off the rod. Delays run out to 7 s; I will use 5 s.
3. Size: three 0.0254 m mounts need an inside diameter of 2 * (0.0254 / sqrt(3) + 0.0254 / 2) = 0.055 m. A 0.058 m tube with a 0.0006 m wall has ID 0.058 - 2 * 0.0006 = 0.0568 m, so the cluster fits. Tube mass pi * 0.058 * 0.0006 * 0.38 * 700 = 0.029 kg. Fins 3 * 0.5 * (0.09 + 0.04) * 0.075 * 0.003 * 160 = 0.007 kg. Nose shell, clay, chute and mounts 0.015 + 0.030 + 0.020 = 0.065 kg. Liftoff 0.029 + 0.007 + 0.065 + 3 * 0.0353 = 0.207 kg, and the motors are 3 * 0.0353 / (0.029 + 0.007 + 0.065 + 3 * 0.0353) = 51% of it. Using 0.21 kg for the round checks, thrust-to-weight is 33.9 / (0.21 * 9.81) = 16.5. Rail acceleration (33.9 - 0.21 * 9.81) / 0.21 = 152 m/s^2, and the exit speed is sqrt(2 * ((33.9 - 0.21 * 9.81) / 0.21) * 0.914) = 16.6 m/s, over the 15 m/s gate on average thrust. Propellant 3 * 0.012 = 0.036 kg, descent mass 0.21 - 0.036 = 0.174 kg. At 4.5 m/s and Cd 0.8 the canopy is 2 * (0.21 - 0.036) * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 4.5^2) = 0.172 m^2, and the diameter is sqrt(4 * (2 * (0.21 - 0.036) * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 4.5^2)) / pi) = 0.47 m. I will use 0.50 m so a heavier build still lands under 5.5 m/s. Burn is 0.78 s, so a 5 s delay opens at 0.78 + 5 = 5.8 s.
4. Draft: 58 mm cardboard tube 0.38 m long, 0.15 m ogive nose, 30 g of clay at the top of the tube, three C11 motors, three modest balsa fins, 0.50 m chute. Flying it on the 0.914 m rod. The clay is there because half the mass is in the tail; I am not waiting to discover an unstable rocket.
