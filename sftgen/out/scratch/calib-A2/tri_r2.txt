2. Feasibility: three C11 motors is 3 * 8.8 = 26.4 N*s, the same impulse as three C6 motors, with 3 * 11.3 = 33.9 N average against 3 * 4.8 = 14.4 N for the C6. The C11 is a 24 mm motor, so the tube has to be wider, and the motors weigh 3 * 0.0353 = 0.106 kg. The B6 is only 4.3 N*s and the only delay listed is 0 s, so it would fire the ejection charge at burnout. Three C11 motors with a 5 s delay are the set that can leave a 0.914 m rod and still coast.
3. Size: tube mass pi * 0.056 * 0.0008 * 0.42 * 700 = 0.041 kg. Three balsa fins 3 * 0.5 * (0.10 + 0.04) * 0.08 * 0.0032 * 160 = 0.0086 kg. Nose, chute, mounts and rings 0.025 + 0.030 = 0.055 kg. Liftoff 0.041 + 0.0086 + 0.055 + 0.106 = 0.211 kg. Thrust-to-weight 33.9 / (0.211 * 9.81) = 16.4. Acceleration on the rail (33.9 - 0.211 * 9.81) / 0.211 = 151 m/s^2, and sqrt(2 * 151 * 0.914) = 16.6 m/s, a little over the 15 m/s gate even before the ignition spike. Propellant 3 * 0.012 = 0.036 kg, so the canopy carries 0.211 - 0.036 = 0.175 kg. For 4.5 m/s at Cd 0.8 the area is 2 * 0.175 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 4.5^2) = 0.173 m^2, and the diameter is sqrt(4 * 0.173 / pi) = 0.469 m. A 0.48 m chute sits just under the 5.5 m/s limit. The burn is 0.78 s, so a 5 s delay opens near 6 s, about when a 305 m flight should be topping out.
