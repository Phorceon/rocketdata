1. Understand: apogee about 125 m (approximate; within 5% means roughly 119 to 131 m). Motor class at most G, an upper limit rather than a requirement, since the flyer has no certification (fixed). No rail length, wind or landing limit is stated, so I assume the 1.5 m rail, 2 m/s wind and the usual 6 m/s touchdown limit. One stage is enough at this altitude.
2. Feasibility: 125 m is a low flight. Ignoring drag it needs v = sqrt(2*9.81*125) = 49.5 m/s at burnout, and a light rocket loses a large share of its ideal height to drag, so the real figure is higher. An A or B motor in a small airframe is the natural size; a C would already overshoot a light build, and using the G ceiling would mean a deliberately heavy rocket, which makes no sense for an uncertified flyer. I need real impulse and thrust data for the small-motor candidates before sizing.
