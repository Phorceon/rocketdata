Feasibility: the C6 returns 8.8 N*s over 1.83 s with a loaded mass of 23.1 g, while the A8 (2.2 N*s) and B6 (4.3 N*s) both come back listing only a 0 s ejection delay, so neither can time a chute deployment near apogee. The C6 in a deliberately stout airframe is the practical pick and it sits far under the G ceiling. As an upper bound, 8.8 N*s on about 0.08 kg gives 8.8 / 0.08 = 110 m/s of ideal delta-v, well above the 49.5 m/s ballistic figure even after heavy drag losses.
3. Size: I am aiming at roughly 0.08 kg loaded: a 41.6 mm tube 0.45 m long, a 0.11 m hollow plastic ogive nose, an 18 mm motor mount and three balsa fins. Thrust-to-weight = 4.8 / (0.08 * 9.81) = 6.1, above the 3:1 minimum. With average thrust alone, a = (4.8 - 0.08 * 9.81) / 0.08 = 50.2 m/s^2 and rail exit would be sqrt(2 * 50.2 * 1.5) = 12.3 m/s, under the 15 m/s gate; but the C6's 14.1 N ignition spike dominates the short rail stroke, so the real exit speed should be well above the constant-thrust figure. Descent mass is about 0.08 - 0.011 = 0.069 kg. For a 5 m/s sink rate under Cd 0.8 the canopy needs A = 2 * 0.069 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 5^2) = 0.055 m^2, a diameter of sqrt(4 * 0.055 / 3.1416) = 0.26 m, so a 0.35 m canopy leaves margin. Burnout is at 1.83 s and apogee should come near 4 s, so the 3 s delay ejects close to the top.
4. Draft: 0.56 m long, 41.6 mm diameter, about 0.08 kg loaded, C6-3 motor, 0.35 m chute, 3 fins at the tail for margin on the short fat tube. Checking it in the simulator.
