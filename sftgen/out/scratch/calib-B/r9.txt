2. Feasibility: the L1400-LW returns 2842.9 N*s over a 2.0 s burn, 1422 N average thrust, 54 mm diameter, 0.736 m long and 2.54 kg loaded — huge thrust for its impulse class. The delay list holds only 0 s, so the motor is effectively plugged and the chute has to go out on an apogee (altimeter) event, which is normal for this size. Feasibility is not in doubt: with this much thrust the whole game is keeping mass and frontal area down while holding a stable margin.
3. Size: the smallest practical airframe for a 54 mm mount is about 0.06 m diameter. Rough build: 1.10 m tube, 0.30 m ogive, about 3.5 kg loaded. Thrust-to-weight = 1422 / (3.5 * 9.81) = 41. Rail exit on average thrust: a = (1422 - 3.5 * 9.81) / 3.5 = 396 m/s^2, so v = sqrt(2 * 396 * 1.5) = 34.5 m/s, far over the gate. Ideal delta-v is 2842.9 / 3.5 = 812 m/s, Mach territory before drag losses, so apogee will land in the several-thousand-metre range wherever the simulator puts it. Descent mass is about 3.5 - 1.4 = 2.1 kg; for a 5 m/s sink under Cd 0.8 the canopy needs A = 2 * 2.1 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 5^2) = 1.68 m^2, a diameter of sqrt(4 * 1.68 / 3.1416) = 1.46 m, so a 1.5 m chute.
