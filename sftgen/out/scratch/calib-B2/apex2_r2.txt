2. Feasibility: J800T is 1264.4 N*s, 665.0 N average, 1.9 s, 0.054 m and 1.0859 kg, with only a 0 delay, so it flies plugged and the electronics eject. J350W is 669.2 N*s in 38 mm, too little impulse for a 76 mm build. J90W is 689.4 N*s but only 97.6 N. On a 2.0 kg rocket that is 97.6 / (2.0 * 9.81) = 5.0, and (97.6 - 2.0 * 9.81) / 2.0 = 39.0 m/s^2 leaves the rail at sqrt(2 * 39.0 * 1.5) = 10.8 m/s, under 15 m/s. K550W at 1624.9 N*s is the modest step up if the J comes up short. K700W and the L730 are there if drag eats more than I think. Starting with the J800T, because it is the smallest motor here that can both lift a fiberglass tube and have a chance at 2,500 m.
3. Size: liftoff about 2.0 kg, burnout 2.0 - 0.61 = 1.39 kg, round the chute math to 1.4 kg. Thrust-to-weight is 665.0 / (2.0 * 9.81) = 33.9. Net acceleration (665.0 - 2.0 * 9.81) / 2.0 = 323 m/s^2, so rail exit is about sqrt(2 * 323 * 1.5) = 31.1 m/s. A 0.30 m drogue falls at sqrt(2 * 1.4 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 3.1416 * 0.15 * 0.15)) = 19.9 m/s. A 1.2 m main falls at sqrt(2 * 1.4 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 3.1416 * 0.6 * 0.6)) = 5.0 m/s. Dropping from 2,500 m to a 150 m main takes (2500 - 150) / 19.9 = 118 s, the main takes 150 / 5.0 = 30 s, and with roughly 25 s to get to apogee the flight is 25 + 118 + 30 = 173 s. That fits in 240 s unless the rocket goes much higher than the minimum.
4. Draft: 76 mm fiberglass, 0.85 m tube, 0.19 m ogive nose, three plywood fins, 54 mm mount, plugged J800T, 50 g for the altimeter, 0.30 m drogue at apogee, 1.2 m main at 150 m. Launch wind 2 m/s.
