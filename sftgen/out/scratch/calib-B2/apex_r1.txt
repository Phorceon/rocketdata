1. Understand: apogee at least 2,500 m. Total flight time at most 4 minutes, which is 4 * 60 = 240 s, so the rocket is back before it can drift far. Dual deploy is allowed. Wind is about 2 m/s. Nothing is said about rail length, launch angle, or landing speed, so I assume a 1.5 m rail, a vertical launch, and the usual 6 m/s touchdown limit. One stage is enough; the builder asked for something they can actually make.
2. Feasibility: a single chute cannot do both jobs. At the 6 m/s landing limit the descent alone from 2,500 m takes 2500 / 6 = 417 s, and the climb is extra, so the flight would run well past 240 s. Dual deploy is required: a small drogue from apogee, then a main low enough that the slow part stays short. The no-drag burnout speed for 2,500 m is sqrt(2 * 9.81 * 2500) = 221 m/s. Drag will eat a large share of that, so I want on the order of 600 to 1,200 N*s in a light 54 mm airframe rather than a huge Level 3 motor. I need real impulse, thrust, mass, and delay data before I pick one.
