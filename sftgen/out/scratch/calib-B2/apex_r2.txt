2. Feasibility: the J800T returns 1264.4 N*s, 665.0 N average, a 1.9 s burn, 0.054 m diameter and 1.0859 kg loaded. That is enough impulse for 2,500 m if the airframe stays near 2 kg, and the thrust will leave a 1.5 m rail quickly. The J90W has a similar 689.4 N*s but only 97.6 N average. At 2.0 kg its thrust-to-weight is 97.6 / (2.0 * 9.81) = 5.0, and the rail-exit estimate is sqrt(2 * 39.0 * 1.5) = 10.8 m/s after (97.6 - 2.0 * 9.81) / 2.0 = 39.0 m/s^2, short of 15 m/s. The J350W and I600R are about half the impulse and are 38 mm motors. The K550W at 1624.9 N*s would clear the altitude with room, but it is more motor than this flight needs, so I am holding it as the backup. The J800T delay list is only 0, so the motor stays plugged and electronics fire the chutes.
3. Size: liftoff mass about 2.0 kg, so burnout mass is 2.0 - 0.61 = 1.39 kg, call it 1.4 kg. Thrust-to-weight is 665.0 / (2.0 * 9.81) = 33.9. Net acceleration is (665.0 - 2.0 * 9.81) / 2.0 = 323 m/s^2, so rail exit is about sqrt(2 * 323 * 1.5) = 31.1 m/s. Drogue area 3.1416 * 0.15 * 0.15 = 0.0707 m^2 gives sqrt(2 * 1.4 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 0.0707)) = 19.9 m/s. Main area 3.1416 * 0.6 * 0.6 = 1.13 m^2 gives sqrt(2 * 1.4 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 1.13)) = 5.0 m/s. From 2,500 m down to a 150 m main, drogue time is (2500 - 150) / 19.9 = 118 s, the main takes 150 / 5.0 = 30 s, and with about 25 s to apogee the flight is 25 + 118 + 30 = 173 s, inside 240 s. A higher apogee eats that margin.
4. Draft: 76 mm fiberglass airframe, 0.85 m tube plus a 0.19 m ogive nose, three plywood fins, a 54 mm mount for the plugged J800T, a 0.30 m drogue at apogee and a 1.2 m main at 150 m. A 50 g altimeter mass stands in for the bay. Flying it in the stated 2 m/s wind.
