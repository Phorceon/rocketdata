5. Fix: 2055.1 m is still short of 2,500 m. The K700W is the lightest step up that might do it, at 2283.7 N*s, but it is another short, heavy burn and the margin would be thin. The 75 mm L850W and L1520T do not fit a 0.073 m tube. The 2765L730-P is a 54 mm plugged reload: 2763.2 N*s, 739.1 N average, 3.74 s burn, 0.649 m long and 2.247 kg. It has more impulse and a longer burn than the J800T, which is what the Mach 1.41 flight was asking for.
Mass added by the casing swap is 2.247 - 1.0859 = 1.16 kg, so 2.08 + 1.16 = 3.24 kg before the longer tube. I am allowing about 0.15 kg for the extra 0.20 m of airframe, 3.24 + 0.15 = 3.39 kg, and sizing to 3.4 kg. Thrust-to-weight is 739.1 / (3.4 * 9.81) = 22.2. Rail acceleration is (739.1 - 3.4 * 9.81) / 3.4 = 208 m/s^2, and sqrt(2 * 208 * 1.5) = 25.0 m/s. Impulse ratio 2763.2 / 1264.4 = 2.19, mass ratio 3.4 / 2.08 = 1.63, and 2.19 / 1.63 = 1.34, so a rough altitude is 2055.1 * 1.34 = 2750 m.
The heavier burnout needs a bigger main. Old burnout was 2.08 - 0.6133 = 1.47 kg. Empty motors are 1.0859 - 0.6133 = 0.473 kg and 2.247 - 1.351 = 0.896 kg, so the new burnout is 1.47 - 0.473 + 0.896 + 0.15 = 2.04 kg. Speed scales by sqrt(2.04 / 1.47) = 1.18, and 5.3 * 1.18 = 6.25 m/s, over the 6 m/s limit. A main of 1.2 * 6.25 / 5.0 = 1.5 m brings that back near 5 m/s. The mount grows to 0.67 m and the tube to 1.05 m so the motor and the chutes both fit. Fins stay as they are.
