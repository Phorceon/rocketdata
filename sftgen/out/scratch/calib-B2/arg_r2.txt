2. Feasibility: L1520T is class L, 3769.0 N*s, 1511.4 N average, 2.49 s, 0.075 m diameter, 0.5179 m long and 3.62 kg. That thrust will lift a loaded stack. L850W is almost the same impulse at 3695.0 N*s but only 868.2 N, and the L730 is a smaller 2763.2 N*s. K700W is class K, 2283.7 N*s, 688.9 N, 3.3 s, 0.054 m and 2.0353 kg. K550W is 1624.9 N*s and K185W is a long 7.1 s burn but only 1378.6 N*s, so the K700W is the sustainer. Together 3769.0 + 2283.7 = 6050 N*s. Both motors list only a 0 delay or none, so both stay plugged and the chutes are electronic. The sustainer lights at booster burnout.
3. Size: motors are 3.62 + 2.04 = 5.7 kg, and a 98 mm fiberglass airframe, nose, fins and two chutes are about 1.8 kg, so liftoff is 5.7 + 1.8 = 7.5 kg. Thrust-to-weight is 1511.4 / (7.5 * 9.81) = 20.5. Off a 2.5 m rail the acceleration is (1511.4 - 7.5 * 9.81) / 7.5 = 192 m/s^2 and the exit speed is sqrt(2 * 192 * 2.5) = 31.0 m/s. Sustainer empty is about 2.04 - 1.29 = 0.75 kg of casing plus structure, call the descending sustainer 1.8 kg. Its 1.8 m chute falls at sqrt(2 * 1.8 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 3.1416 * 0.9 * 0.9)) = 3.76 m/s. Booster empty is 3.62 - 1.77 = 1.85 kg plus its tube and fins, about 2.7 kg, and a 2.2 m chute falls at sqrt(2 * 2.7 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 3.1416 * 1.1 * 1.1)) = 3.77 m/s. Both are under 4.5 m/s. Chutes open at each section's own apogee so they are not out while the stack is still supersonic.
4. Draft: both stages 98 mm fiberglass. Sustainer has a 0.28 m ogive nose, a 0.95 m tube, the K700W, three plywood fins and the 1.8 m chute. Booster is a 0.85 m tube, the L1520T, larger plywood fins and the 2.2 m chute, separating at burnout. Wind 5.6 m/s, rail 2.5 m.
