1. Understand: the flight is for 8,500 m. Nothing says at least or at most, so I am treating 8,500 m as the target and staying inside 5% of it. No motor, diameter, wind, rail or landing speed is given. I assume a 1.5 m rail, 2 m/s wind, a vertical sea-level launch and a 6 m/s touchdown. One stage unless the motor data says otherwise. A club rocket, so a single certified motor rather than a research stack.
2. Feasibility: with no drag, 8,500 m needs sqrt(2 * 9.81 * 8500) = 408 m/s at burnout. Drag will take a large share, so this is an M or N motor in a 75 to 98 mm tube, not a K. A single chute at 6 m/s would take 8500 / 6 = 1417 s just to come down, which is a long time to be in even a light wind. Dual deploy is the practical way to land under 6 m/s without spending half an hour in the air. I need impulse, thrust, mass and diameter for a few M and N motors before I size the tube.
