2. Feasibility: the C6 gives 8.8 N*s over 1.83 s and the D12 gives 16.8 N*s. Roughly doubling the impulse would take a light D rocket far past 320 m, so the D12 only makes sense if the rocket is heavy. A C6 in a BT-50 airframe is the closer match, and it stays inside the D limit.
3. Size: estimated liftoff mass 0.055 kg with the motor loaded. Thrust-to-weight = 4.8 / (0.055 * 9.81) = 8.9, well above 3. Average acceleration on the rail is about (4.8 - 0.54) / 0.055 = 77 m/s^2, so rail exit is near sqrt(2 * 77 * 1.5) = 15.2 m/s. That is marginal against 15 m/s, but the C6 thrust spike at ignition should push it higher. Descent mass is about 0.055 - 0.011 = 0.044 kg. For a 4 m/s descent with Cd 0.8, the canopy area is 2 * 0.044 * 9.81 / (1.225 * 0.8 * 4^2) = 0.055 m^2, which is a diameter of sqrt(4 * 0.055 / 3.1416) = 0.26 m. A 12 in (0.305 m) chute covers that. Burnout comes at about 1.8 s, and a small rocket coasts for several seconds after that, so I'll take the 5 s delay.
4. Draft: 24.9 mm BT-50 body, 0.46 m long, with an ogive nose, three balsa fins and an 18 mm motor mount. Checking it in the simulator, with the stated 2.2 m/s wind.
